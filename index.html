What the quarter looked like

  1. +18.1%

    Staking grew

    We counted 754.8 million RBNT in the two Reddex pools at the end of September, 115.7 million more than at the end of June. The no lock pool took 82.6 million of that change. Open stakers: 1,156 to 1,140 in the no lock pool, 223 to 217 in the 365 day pool.

  2. 127.8 million

    Vesting contracts paid out

    The 18 contracts whose published code is a token vesting manager sent 127.8 million WRBNT to unnamed wallets in 1,885 transfers. Together they went from 1.68 billion to 1.58 billion.

  3. 786.4 million

    Reserved wallets reshuffled among themselves

    Three moves inside the reserved group: Ecosystem (Locked) to Ecosystem 2 (Unlocked), 500 million; Ecosystem 2 (Unlocked) to Investors, 253.1 million; Governance (Locked) to Governance (Unlocked), 33.3 million. Ecosystem (Unlocked) was the only one to send RBNT outside the group (43.8 million), mostly into WRBNT and the pools' reward reserves.

  4. +45.3 million

    The biggest wallets ended up with more

    We ranked unnamed wallets on 30 June and followed the top 100. They held 1.38 billion RBNT then (wrapped and staked included) and 1.42 billion on 30 September. 43 grew by over 1%, 49 stayed within 1% and 8 shrank by over 1%.

  5. 48.4%

    Holdings stayed top-heavy

    The ten largest wallets hold 48.4% of everything unnamed wallets hold, up from 47.5%. The largest 1% of wallets (535 of them) hold 93.5%. A typical wallet, the one in the middle, holds 5.84 RBNT.

  6. +157

    A few more holders

    53,575 wallets held at least 1 RBNT on 30 September against 53,418 on 30 June. Behind that net figure, 243 wallets rose past 1 RBNT and 86 dropped under it.

Public names only

Twelve addresses are named: the nine reserved wallets published by the DAO, the WRBNT contract and Reddex's two RBNT pools. The explorer tags no exchange wallet, so we name none.

Groups of five or more

Every other address is only ever part of a count or a total, in a group with at least four others. No address outside the named list appears anywhere.

What, not why

The chain shows coins moving between addresses. It cannot show who is behind an address or the reason for a move. We forecast nothing and advise nothing.

Three terms used throughout

Address
A public account on the chain. Anyone can look up what it holds.
Wallet or contract
A wallet is opened with a private key held by a person or an organisation. A contract is a program that holds and moves coins by fixed rules.
Holding
Everything an address owns: RBNT, plus WRBNT, plus RBNT it has staked. Wrapped and staked coins are counted once, with their owner, and never again at the contract.
01

The 10 billion, group by group

Short answerAt the end of September Redbelly's reserved wallets held 59.3% of all RBNT. Unnamed wallets held 19.6% and contracts outside the named list 21.1%, most of it in token vesting contracts.

Every RBNT that exists was minted when the network launched, so the total never changes. What changes is where the coins are. In chart 1 each holding is credited to its owner, so RBNT that a wallet has wrapped or staked still counts as that wallet's.

How we sort contracts

When a contract's author publishes its source code on the explorer, the code carries a name such as TokenVestingManager or GnosisSafe. We use that name to put the contract in a broad kind, and we only show a kind when at least five contracts share it. A token vesting contract releases coins to fixed recipients on a timetable. A shared-key wallet (Safe) needs several keys to approve a payment. An exchange liquidity pool is the stock of coins people swap against on a decentralised exchange.

Counting by the address that holds the coins

Chart 2 adds up the same coins differently: by the address that physically holds them. Here the WRBNT contract and the two pools show up as places, because that is where wrapped and staked RBNT actually sit.

Did we find every coin?

Yes, to within 36,485 RBNT: the balances we read add up to 99.9996% of the supply on 30 September. The rest sits at addresses that never took part in any transaction, internal transfer or block header we read, most likely funded at launch and never touched. That amount moved by 11 RBNT over the quarter, far too little to affect any trend.

Where the coins went during the quarter

Chart 3 traces every transfer of RBNT and WRBNT between groups from 1 July to 30 September. The four biggest streams were stakes from wallets into the pools (196.4 million), WRBNT from vesting contracts to wallets (127.8 million), RBNT leaving the WRBNT contract as wallets unwrapped (115.9 million), and withdrawals plus rewards paid out of the pools (95.7 million). Moves inside one group are kept out of the picture and listed in the table.

02

Big and small holders

Short answer53,575 wallets held 1 RBNT or more at the end of September. 89% of them hold under 100 RBNT, while 23 wallets with 10 million or more RBNT hold 60% of the total.

These counts cover unnamed wallets only. Reserved wallets and contracts are left out because they are large by design and would drown out everyone else. Wallets with less than 1 RBNT are left out too: there were 111,509 of them on 30 September, mostly holding fee change.

Reading this carefully

A top-heavy spread means a small set of addresses could move a large part of the supply. It says nothing about who those addresses belong to. An exchange can keep the coins of thousands of people in one wallet, and one person can spread coins over many wallets.

04

Did the big wallets add or cut?

Short answerOn balance they added. The 21 wallets that held 10 million or more RBNT on 30 June had 1.06 billion between them then and 1.09 billion three months later; 9 of them grew, 7 held flat and 5 shrank.

We never follow a single wallet. Instead we sort wallets into groups by what they held on 30 June, keep each group fixed, and count what its members held on 30 September. A wallet counts as up if its holding grew by more than 1%, down if it shrank by more than 1%, and flat in between. Staking or wrapping coins is not a fall, since the holding includes both.

A word of caution on the small groups: their net change can be driven by a handful of wallets that jumped several sizes in one quarter. The movement table further down shows how many wallets changed size, and in which direction.

05

Staking in Reddex

Short answerUp 18.1%: the two Reddex pools held 639.1 million RBNT on 30 June and 754.8 million on 30 September.

Reddex runs two pools for RBNT. In the no lock pool a staker can take coins back at any time. In the 365 day pool each deposit stays put for a year from the day it was made. Following the brief, our headline numbers are the RBNT each pool held at the two dates.

Why a pool's balance is not all stakes

Stakers are paid their rewards in RBNT straight out of the pool's own coins. So a pool's balance is two things mixed together: what stakers put in, and a reserve kept for paying rewards. The pool contract keeps its own running total of stakes, called totalStake. Subtracting it from the balance gives the reward reserve, and we show both.

Exchanges and other tagged addresses

The explorer shows a public name on just one of the 20,000 largest addresses we read from its list: Wrapped RBNT. It tags no exchange wallet, so we name none. Any counterparty without a public name shows up only as a group: unnamed wallets, or contracts by kind.

Wrapped RBNT

WRBNT is RBNT packaged as a token so that apps built for tokens can use it. The WRBNT contract keeps one RBNT for each WRBNT in circulation; we checked this held at every snapshot. Over the quarter the amount of WRBNT outstanding went from 1.82 billion to 1.74 billion, a change of -82.2 million.

07

How we did it

Short answerWe read the whole chain ourselves, took every balance at fixed blocks, and had a second program read the published numbers back from the node before release.

  • Fixed blocks. Each date stands for the newest block stamped before midnight UTC: block 3,025,809 for the end of 30 June and block 3,209,414 for the end of 30 September. Month ends and every day of the quarter follow the same rule.
  • The whole chain. We read all 3,209,415 blocks from launch to the end block and 1,753,030 internal transfers, which turned up 761,594 addresses that could hold RBNT.
  • Balances from the node. For each address we read RBNT, WRBNT and staked RBNT at the snapshot blocks. We never use the balances shown in the explorer's lists, which are current and rounded.
  • Exact checks. 58 checks must hold to the wei before any table is written, among them: the WRBNT supply equals the sum of all WRBNT balances; each pool's stakers add up to its totalStake; stakes minus withdrawals equal the change in totalStake; every group adds back to the coins found; every named wallet balances to the last wei.
  • A second read. After the tables are built, a separate stage reads every named balance, both pools' totalStake and the WRBNT supply again straight from the node, plus 600 balances picked at random, and scans every published file for any address outside the named list.

Try it yourself

Paste either command into a terminal to read a figure from this report directly from the chain.

# RBNT held by the Reserve wallet at the end of the quarter (answer in wei, divide by 10^18)
curl -s https://governors.mainnet.redbelly.network -H 'content-type: application/json' \
  -d '{"jsonrpc":"2.0","id":1,"method":"eth_getBalance","params":["0x7473fEAf836b5319D9190d8367991Dc6ef33C16d","0x30f8c6"]}'

# Staked RBNT (totalStake) in the 365 day pool at the end of the quarter
curl -s https://governors.mainnet.redbelly.network -H 'content-type: application/json' \
  -d '{"jsonrpc":"2.0","id":1,"method":"eth_call","params":[{"to":"0x5E8040e85D0E6363D798a43BEa939C026449946d","data":"0x8b0e9f3f"},"0x30f8c6"]}'

The full method, the list of sources and the steps for the next edition are on How we did it. The code that produced every number sits in the same repository as this page.

What the chain cannot tell us

  • Who controls an address, or why coins moved. A group's holdings can fall because someone sold, moved coins to another address of their own, or put them into a contract.
  • How many people there are. Counts here are counts of addresses: one person can run many, and one exchange address can hold coins for many people.
  • Internal transfers come from the explorer because the public node offers no trace interface. We tested them indirectly: the named wallets, the WRBNT contract and both pools, which move coins mostly through internal transfers, all balance to the wei.
  • Public tags were read for the 20,000 largest addresses in the explorer's list. A tag on a smaller address would not be seen.

Glossary

TermWhat it means here
RBNTThe Redbelly network's own coin. 10 billion exist, all created at launch.
AddressAn account number on the chain; what it holds is visible to everyone.
WalletAn address whose owner signs with a private key; it may be a person or an organisation.
ContractAn address that is a program and handles coins by fixed rules.
HoldingRBNT plus WRBNT plus staked RBNT belonging to one address.
WRBNTThe token form of RBNT. Each one is matched by an RBNT locked in the WRBNT contract.
StakingDepositing RBNT in a pool to earn rewards, which are paid in RBNT.
totalStakeA pool contract's own running total of the RBNT its stakers have deposited.
Reward reserveRBNT in a pool beyond its totalStake, used to pay rewards.
Reserved walletsThe nine Redbelly addresses published on the DAO treasury page, covering the team, investors, the reserve, ecosystem funds, governance and USYD/CSIRO.
Snapshot blockThe block that stands for a date: the newest one stamped before midnight UTC.
Gini indexOne number for how uneven holdings are, from 0 (all equal) to 1 (one holder has all).
WeiThe smallest unit of RBNT; 1 RBNT is 1018 wei. Our checks are exact to the wei.
